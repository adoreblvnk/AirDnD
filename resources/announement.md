On the three-month track, 24 teams have been building since 21 June on the Little Red Dome challenge - how to defend Singapore with cheap interceptors and agentic command and control systems; and the Neo-Battalion challenge - how ground forces keep pace on a drone-dominated battlefield. On the two-day track, teams get 48 hours on similar challenges but with a greater focus on the tropical environment, robotics and sense making: Green Corridor, Creative Sensing, Interceptors, and One Picture, Many Eyes.

They are competing for more than S$50,000 in cash across 15 prizes, including our Lord of the Rings prize for teams that combine their technologies into one capability, because defence forces prefer buying integrated solutions. Selected teams go on to the NUS Defence Venture Lab (DVL) with S$100,000 in cash and in-kind support.

I want to thank the mentors separately, because they are not on any banner. Some are serving. Some are retired. Some have nothing to do with defence. They have given up evenings and weekends since June to meet with these teams, inspired by young people who want to hack for defence.

All understand what recent conflicts have shown us. Where the field once held soldiers and the skies held manned fighters, both are increasingly filled with robots and drones. This is the scenario our teams are building for, and the mentors wanted to pass on their experience.

Thank you to every partner, mentor and hacker because you believe in our mission of building national resilience through a defence tech ecosystem.
