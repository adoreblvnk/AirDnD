D I S T R I B U T I O N

PARTICIPATING TEAMS

Something changed in the last four years, and every defence ministry on earth is  still  catching  up  with  it.  Mass,  budget  and  pedigree  stopped  deciding outcomes. A two-thousand-dollar quadcopter, built by volunteers, now does the work of a thirteen-million-dollar missile. The advantage has moved to whoever can think, build and field fastest, and it is not moving back.

For  Singapore  that  is  the  best  news  in  a  generation  and  the  most  dangerous.  We cannot win a war of depth - we have fifty kilometres and no rear area. We cannot win a war of stockpiles, because what we would need most is exactly what nobody will sell us at the moment we need it. What we can win is a war of adaptation, and that contest is decided by engineers.

Which is why you are here. The four problems that follow are not exercises. They are unsolved  by  anyone,  and  they  are  the  ones  that  decide  whether  this  island  holds. Nobody expects you to finish one in a weekend. We expect you to see it clearly, come at it from an angle nobody has tried, and show us that it works.

Forty-eight hours. Pick your problem and begin.

## §1 THE FOUR CHALLENGES

## 01 GREEN CORRIDOR

Ideate a machine that takes a dangerous job off a person in Singapore's terrain - jungle, mud, coast or tidal ground. What it does is your decision. Showcase a proof of concept that  makes  the  mechanism  believable:  a  bench  rig,  a  simulation,  or  one  subsystem demonstrated. See document 01 for more details.

## 02 CREATIVE SENSING

Ideate creative ways to detect something that emits no radio, returns almost nothing to radar, and sits at the temperature of the air around it. Showcase a demo that reports how often it cries wolf against a real background - not only how far it can see. See document 02 for more details.

## 03 I N T E R C E P T O R S

Ideate one layer of an autonomous interceptor swarm that needs no pilot - the drone itself,  the  terminal  guidance,  the  swarm  deployment  logic,  or  the  patrol  and  recharge cycle  that  keeps  it  airborne.  Build  it  in  software  and  showcase  it  working,  measured against a stated baseline. See document 03 for more details.

## 04 ONE PICTURE, MANY EYES

Ideate a way to fuse sources nobody has joined - sensors, open data, and things that were never meant to be sensors - into usable warning, either that a conflict is forming or  of  what  is  on  our  approaches  now.  Showcase  a  software  demo  that  turns  many disagreeing  inputs  into  one  picture  somebody  can  act  on.  See  document  04  for  more details.
